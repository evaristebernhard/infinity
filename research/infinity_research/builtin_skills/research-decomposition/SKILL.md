# Decompose research

Read the frozen goal, acceptance criteria, available tools, current work graph,
and existing results before proposing tasks. Ask what would count as a useful
artifact: a measured program, a feasible object, a counterexample, a proof, or
a sourced answer. Keep unresolved interpretation questions explicit.

1. Establish the smallest runnable baseline or checkable example.
2. Propose independent routes with different mechanisms. Give each task a
   concrete input, expected artifact, completion check, dependencies, and the
   relevant skill names. Delegate a unit of work, not a vague persona.
3. Put shared prerequisites before their consumers. Keep a join task for
   integration; completion of several fragments is not completion of the goal.
4. Claim a ready task before doing it. Pass artifact references and short
   evidence summaries to other workers rather than whole conversations.
5. After a result, update the graph: repair an execution failure, abandon a
   contradicted assumption, deepen a promising route, or explore an independent
   route. Avoid repeatedly issuing the same task with different wording.

Leave a durable handoff with what ran, what was learned, exact artifact IDs,
open questions, and the next useful action. A recovered worker must be able to
continue from that handoff. If no task is ready, identify the blocking dependency;
do not invent progress or claim another worker's leased task.

Use the host's actual acceptance result when closing work. A plausible narrative,
agent agreement, or a completed work item does not verify a scientific claim.
